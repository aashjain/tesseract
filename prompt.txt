# AG Designs - The AG Tesseract

## Claude-ready production plan for an immersive 3D storytelling homepage

Version: 1.0  
Prepared from: `AG_Designs_Concept_01_Tesseract.pdf`  
Primary creative benchmark: Lusion's commitment to scroll-led 3D visual storytelling, motion craft and technical polish - not its layouts, assets, copy, scene ideas or visual identity.

> Naming decision before production: the client deck consistently says "AG Designs," while the request refers to "AG Design Studios." Confirm the public-facing name, wordmark and legal footer before writing final copy. This document uses "AG Designs" to match the supplied deck.

---

## 1. Executive direction

### The big idea

**The AG Tesseract: a system for making signals unmistakable.**

The visitor begins inside a fragmented field of brand activity: posts, colours, captions, ideas, frames and signals that exist but do not add up. The "A" in AG appears as an aperture and pulls the visitor into a multidimensional creative system. Inside it, strategy gives the fragments direction, identity gives them a recognisable form, content gives them momentum, production makes them tangible, digital design gives people a way in, and campaigns send the finished system outward. Work is encountered as evidence embedded in this transformation. At the end, every loose element aligns into a coherent brand world. The camera pulls back to reveal a completed orbit shaped by the "G": not a second logo trick, but the visual consequence of a system that now has gravity.

### One-line website proposition

**We turn scattered brand activity into a world people can recognise, enter and remember.**

### Internal creative manifesto

AG Designs does not add more content to the noise. It finds the organising idea. Every decision - strategy, identity, social, production, digital and campaign - becomes part of one recognisable system. The experience must therefore behave like the work: curious at first, rigorous underneath, human in its details, and unmistakably coherent by the end.

### Visitor transformation

| Arrival state | Departure state |
|---|---|
| Present, but not positioned | A clear and defensible position |
| Publishing disconnected content | Communicating with purpose |
| Many ideas without a system | One recognisable identity with range |
| Visibility as the goal | Meaningful recognition as the goal |
| Services perceived as outputs | AG perceived as an integrated creative partner |

### Creative guardrails

- Premium, editorial, intelligent and slightly surreal.
- Abstract creative-space language, not literal outer space.
- No rockets, astronauts, planets, generic star fields, cyberpunk corridors or gratuitous neon.
- No ordinary row of service cards or tiled portfolio grid on the cinematic route.
- No camera movement without narrative purpose.
- No imitation of Lusion scenes, compositions, writing, transitions, shaders or asset design.
- Copy stays concise, conversational and specific. Avoid every generic agency phrase listed in the deck.
- The HTML experience remains a complete website. WebGL enriches meaning but never contains the only copy, navigation or call to action.

## 2. Narrative architecture

### Story model

The homepage is a guided story with optional branches. Scroll controls forward and backward time. Hover, pointer and tap reveal detail, but never gate the main story. A persistent navigation layer lets visitors leave the cinematic route, jump to a capability, open work or contact AG at any point.

The complete journey should take approximately 90 to 150 seconds when scrolled at a comfortable pace. Use 12 core scenes, grouped into four acts:

1. **Act I - Fragmentation:** See the noise, find the aperture, cross the threshold.
2. **Act II - The organising system:** Reveal the tesseract and give the brand direction, form and connected motion.
3. **Act III - Making and proof:** Convert ideas into production, digital worlds, campaigns and evidence.
4. **Act IV - Human resolution:** Reveal the people behind the system, align the brand universe and invite a conversation.

### Emotional progression

| Story beat | Intended feeling | Interaction character |
|---|---|---|
| Noise field | Curiosity, then slight disorientation | Loose, reactive, unresolved |
| Aperture | Discovery | Focused, magnetic |
| Tesseract reveal | Possibility | Expansive, dimensional |
| Strategy and identity | Clarity | Precise, aligning |
| Content, production and digital | Momentum | Energetic, responsive |
| Work and team | Confidence | Concrete, human |
| Resolution and CTA | Excitement | Calm, complete, inviting |

### Role of A and G

- **A = Aperture.** It is an opening and a decision point, introduced early and used as the portal into AG's way of thinking. Its shape must come from the approved left-hand A path in the supplied logo SVG, not a font, a generic triangle or a newly drawn letter.
- **G = Gravity/orbit.** Its long vertical and curved lower stroke already provides the correct spatial language for an orbital resolution. Do not preview it as another doorway. Allow the approved right-hand G path to emerge only in the final transformation when the system has gained coherence. The audience should notice the G shape rather than be told to admire a logo trick.
- **Full monogram = resolved system.** The exact A and G can appear together in the loader at very low emphasis, in the persistent brand mark, and at the final lockup. Do not stamp the full logo into every scene.

### Approved logo integration update

The supplied package contains:

- `1AG Logo.svg` - the authoritative, resolution-independent source.
- `AG Logo - Transparent.png` - a 5000 x 5000 transparent reference/raster fallback.

The SVG uses a `0 0 3750 3750` viewBox. Its first group contains the two monogram glyphs; its second group contains the small curved `designs` wordmark detail. The SVG has no fixed fill colour, so it inherits black/current fill unless the implementation assigns an approved colour.

Do not edit the coordinates, replace either path with typography, redraw the corners, regularise the curve or change the relative proportions. If optimisation is required, keep the untouched source SVG and compare the optimised output visually and geometrically before use.

#### Logo usage through the story

1. **Persistent identity:** Use the full approved SVG as the navigation/home mark. Preserve its square viewBox and safe area. The tiny curved `designs` detail may be hidden only at sizes where it becomes illegible, using an approved monogram-only variant derived from the same SVG.
2. **Opening fragments:** Use cropped samples of the real A horizontal/vertical geometry and G curve as abstract clues. Never show the complete G this early.
3. **A aperture:** Isolate the exact approved A path and use its counter-space as the passage. The portal may gain depth, bevel, translucent edges and lighting, but its front silhouette must remain pixel-faithful to the SVG.
4. **Tesseract rails:** Derive recurring right angles, inner/outer rectangular proportions and corner relationships from the A geometry. This makes the visual world feel born from the identity rather than decorated with it.
5. **G resolution:** Animate existing rails and project arcs onto the exact approved G silhouette. The final camera position must make the G legible before the complete monogram locks up.
6. **Final lockup:** Resolve to the untouched full SVG, including the `designs` wordmark detail where size allows. This is the only moment where the identity should feel completely still.

#### 2D-to-3D technical treatment

- Store the untouched originals under a dedicated `public/brand/source/` directory and commit them.
- Create named, derived assets such as `AGLogoFull`, `AGGlyphA` and `AGGlyphG`; document that they come from the approved path data above.
- Use the original SVG for header, footer, metadata and final 2D lockup. Use derived geometry only for the spatial narrative.
- Generate 3D fronts by loading or converting the exact path. Use shallow extrusion and a conservative bevel that does not alter the front-facing silhouette.
- Keep the A and G on separate scene groups with consistent origin, scale and pivot metadata. Do not manually position unlabelled path fragments in multiple files.
- Test the opening, aperture close-up, mobile fallback and final lockup against the supplied transparent PNG.
- Do not skew, stretch, mirror or arbitrarily rotate the logo. Do not apply unapproved gradients or textures to the final brand lockup.
- Ask for brand approval before changing colour, isolation rules, clear space or use of the curved `designs` detail.

## 3. Opening sequence - first 30 seconds

### 0-4 seconds: An unresolved signal

- Begin with near-black ink, not a busy loading screen.
- A restrained status line reads `Tuning the signal 00-100` while a few typographic fragments drift in different planes.
- The real HTML headline is present behind the loader for accessibility and fast first paint.
- Sound is off by default. Show a small `Sound on` control after the first user gesture.

### 4-10 seconds: Presence without recognition

- Fragments accelerate into view: a cropped caption, a colour chip, a frame corner, an image plane, a cursor trail, a line of strategy language, a play icon.
- None stays legible for long. They compete for the same space.
- On-screen copy: **`Present everywhere.`** followed two beats later by **`Recognised nowhere?`**
- Pointer movement disturbs nearby fragments by a maximum of 12 px. On touch, fragments react to scroll velocity instead.

### 10-17 seconds: A pattern appears

- Thin alignment guides begin finding correspondences between fragments.
- Negative space in the centre resolves into the outline of the AG "A".
- Copy: **`There is a way through.`**
- Prompt: desktop `Move to focus`; touch/keyboard `Continue`.

### 17-23 seconds: The A becomes an aperture

- The A gains shallow depth and a translucent edge material. It should feel fabricated and editorial, not like a glowing sci-fi portal.
- Scrolling moves the camera closer; pointer only affects the viewing angle.
- At 70 percent approach, surrounding noise is pulled toward the opening as curved ribbons.
- Prompt: **`Enter A`**. The button must also be a real focusable control that advances to the next scene.

### 23-30 seconds: Crossing the threshold

- The camera passes through the counter-space of the A.
- Fragments stretch into clean lines, then fold into four-dimensional axes.
- Use one continuous visual cause: the same chaotic elements become the structure of the tesseract.
- Sound, if enabled: a soft broadband texture narrowing into a single tonal interval.
- Final reveal copy: **`Inside, every idea has a place.`**

## 4. Scene-by-scene storyboard

Scene progress values are authoring guides, not hard-coded percentages. Define named timeline labels so scene lengths can change without rewriting camera logic.

### Scene 1 - The Fragment Field

- **Story purpose:** Make the client's problem felt before explaining services.
- **Environment:** Deep ink field with floating, cropped brand artefacts. Use off-axis planes, glyph fragments, broken paths and misaligned colour systems. Avoid literal stars.
- **Interaction:** Pointer creates a subtle repulsion field. Scroll reveals more fragments, then exposes the lack of connection between them.
- **Visitor learns:** Activity is not the same as a brand system.
- **On-screen copy:** `Present everywhere.` / `Recognised nowhere?`
- **Transition:** Alignment guides reveal the negative-space A.

### Scene 2 - The A Aperture

- **Story purpose:** Introduce AG as the route from noise to direction.
- **Environment:** The exact approved left-hand A SVG path becomes a shallow dimensional object. Its rectangular counter-space is the literal passage. Use a restrained violet-to-cyan rim without changing the front silhouette, proportions or counter.
- **Interaction:** Scroll approaches; pointer adjusts parallax; keyboard/touch users can activate `Enter A`.
- **Visitor learns:** AG begins by changing the way the problem is framed.
- **On-screen copy:** `There is a way through.` / `Enter A.`
- **Transition:** Loose fragments stretch through the aperture and become ordered rails.

### Scene 3 - The Tesseract Revealed

- **Story purpose:** Reveal the central brand world and explain that the capabilities operate as one system.
- **Environment:** A nested cube/tesseract made from slim architectural rails, translucent membranes and moving coordinates. The space has depth but remains editorial and legible.
- **Interaction:** Scroll performs a 90-degree dimensional fold, not a full camera roll. Hovering a rail previews a service name. The guided path remains dominant.
- **Visitor learns:** Strategy, identity, content, production, digital and campaigns are connected dimensions.
- **On-screen copy:** `Not seven services.` / `One connected system.`
- **Transition:** One rail straightens and becomes a directional vector.

### Scene 4 - Axes of Intent

- **Capability:** Brand strategy and positioning.
- **Story purpose:** Give the previously directionless universe a point of view.
- **Environment:** A navigational instrument made from axes, constraints, audience nodes and a single emerging north line. Use language fragments such as audience, category, tension, promise and proof.
- **Interaction:** As the visitor scrolls, competing vectors collapse into one. Hovering nodes exposes a short process annotation: `Understand`, `Choose`, `Position`.
- **Visitor learns:** AG decides what the brand should mean before deciding how it should look.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Direction before decoration.`**  
  Support: `We find the position every expression can build from.`
- **Transition:** The north line traces a recognisable silhouette and becomes an identity grid.

### Scene 5 - The Identity Engine

- **Capability:** Branding and visual identity.
- **Story purpose:** Convert strategic direction into a distinctive, flexible visual grammar.
- **Environment:** Type, marks, colour fields, spacing modules and image treatments orbit separately, then lock into a responsive identity system.
- **Interaction:** Pointer shifts between two controlled identity states, showing consistency with range. A `View the system` focusable hotspot opens a short project proof.
- **Visitor learns:** Branding is a repeatable system, not a single logo.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Recognition, by design.`**  
  Support: `A system distinct enough to own. Flexible enough to grow.`
- **Transition:** Identity modules emit pulses that connect distant nodes.

### Scene 6 - The Signal Constellation

- **Capabilities:** Social media strategy and management; campaign planning, launches and growth communication.
- **Story purpose:** Show purposeful distribution across channels and moments.
- **Environment:** A network of channel nodes connected by coordinated signal paths. Campaign waves expand outward only after the core message is aligned.
- **Interaction:** Scroll advances a message through the network. Hover/tap a node to see its role, not vanity metrics. A campaign node expands into a launch sequence: tease, reveal, sustain.
- **Visitor learns:** AG plans what each channel should do and how the whole system compounds.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Every signal, connected.`**  
  Support: `Right idea. Right rhythm. One recognisable voice.`
- **Transition:** Signal pulses become moving frames and an editorial content strip.

### Scene 7 - The Momentum Field

- **Capability:** Content creation, reels and campaign concepts.
- **Story purpose:** Demonstrate that content is the repeated movement of the brand system.
- **Environment:** Story beats, hooks, frames, supers and edits flow along a curved timeline. Replace fake social-feed cards with abstract but recognisable production grammar.
- **Interaction:** Scroll scrubs a three-beat reel storyboard. Pointer hover briefly freezes a frame and reveals `Hook`, `Build`, `Payoff`.
- **Visitor learns:** AG creates content with a job to do, not content for volume.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Ideas that keep moving.`**  
  Support: `Built for attention. Connected to the brand.`
- **Transition:** Frames gain depth and enter a light stage.

### Scene 8 - The Matter Lab

- **Capability:** Photography, video and production.
- **Story purpose:** Move from conceptual system to tangible execution.
- **Environment:** A minimal virtual studio with light cards, a subject plinth, lens planes and material swatches. Abstract frames become real client photography/video thumbnails when content is available.
- **Interaction:** Scroll changes the lighting setup from flat idea to resolved shot. Hovering the light rig reveals planning, direction, capture and post-production.
- **Visitor learns:** AG carries the strategic idea through craft and execution.
- **On-screen copy:** **`From thought to thing.`**  
  Support: `Planned, directed, captured and finished as one idea.`
- **Transition:** The final camera frame opens into a portal.

### Scene 9 - The Portal Stack

- **Capability:** Website and digital experience design.
- **Story purpose:** Show digital experiences as worlds people can enter, not screens placed on a device mockup.
- **Environment:** Layered portals, each with a different interaction rule, depth treatment and content architecture. One portal uses the current AG experience as a recursive example only if visually clean.
- **Interaction:** Scroll moves through one portal. Hover/tap previews another project's interface and provides a focusable `Enter case study` link.
- **Visitor learns:** AG makes digital systems that express the brand and help users act.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Worlds people can enter.`**  
  Support: `Clear to use. Distinct enough to remember.`
- **Transition:** Portals rotate outward into an evidence archive.

### Scene 10 - The Evidence Chamber

- **Story purpose:** Turn portfolio into proof encountered inside the narrative.
- **Environment:** Three to five project artefacts float inside separate frames, each with a distinct spatial silhouette. No infinite carousel and no grid on the main route.
- **Interaction:** Focus, hover or tap opens a case-study layer with five items in this order: client, challenge, strategic idea, created work, outcome. The URL becomes `/work/[slug]` or updates with an intercepting route so the item is linkable and browser-back works.
- **Visitor learns:** AG's approach produces specific work and measurable or observable outcomes.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Proof, not promises.`**  
  Support: `Enter a project. Follow the thinking.`
- **Transition:** Project frames flip to reveal notes, hands, faces and working moments.

### Scene 11 - The Human Node

- **Story purpose:** Prevent the system from feeling cold or anonymous.
- **Environment:** Warm off-white light enters the dark world. Team portraits, candid process footage, annotations and short beliefs sit on tactile planes. Do not create floating head cutouts.
- **Interaction:** Slow lateral drift controlled by scroll. Hovering a portrait reveals one human sentence rather than a long biography. Keyboard order follows visual order.
- **Visitor learns:** AG is strategically sharp, curious, collaborative, playful and human.
- **On-screen copy:** **`Systems, made by people.`**  
  Support: `Curious minds. Clear opinions. No black-box process.`
- **Transition:** Human annotations become connection lines that pull every earlier fragment back into frame.

### Scene 12 - The G Resolution

- **Story purpose:** Resolve the transformation and invite contact.
- **Environment:** Every earlier object returns in an organised composition: strategy axes, identity modules, signals, content frames, studio light and portals. The camera pulls back. Rails and arcs align onto the exact approved right-hand G path, including its long vertical, inward bar and lower curve. Once the G reads clearly, the untouched A joins it and the full approved monogram resolves, with the small curved `designs` detail shown only if legible at the final size.
- **Interaction:** Forward scroll completes alignment; reverse scroll genuinely disassembles it. Pointer parallax drops almost to zero so the ending feels calm and assured.
- **Visitor learns:** AG does not deliver disconnected outputs. It creates clarity, consistency and an ownable brand world.
- **Primary on-screen copy:** **`Build a world only your brand can own.`**
- **CTA:** `Start a project` with secondary `Explore our work`.
- **Transition:** Contact opens as a normal route or accessible dialog. The completed system remains as a still background.

## 5. Service discovery and portfolio UX

### Guided route plus free exploration

- Default behavior is guided scroll; all visitors see the full story without needing to hunt for hotspots.
- At the Tesseract Reveal, show a compact `Explore dimensions` control. It opens a semantic list of seven capabilities while the matching 3D rail highlights.
- Selecting a capability scrolls to its named timeline label and updates the URL hash, for example `/#strategy` or `/#production`.
- Each scene contains one optional proof hotspot. It opens a relevant project, process note or outcome without blocking progress.
- The menu also exposes Services as a normal HTML index for users who want direct access.

### Project portals

Every featured project must communicate, in this exact hierarchy:

1. Client and project title.
2. The business or communication challenge.
3. AG's strategic idea.
4. What AG created, tagged to relevant capabilities.
5. Result or impact, with a source and date for every numeric claim.

The cinematic layer may preview projects spatially, but the case-study content itself should use standard semantic HTML, real headings, captions, links and media controls. A dedicated `/work` route may use an editorial list or index because usability outweighs the "no grid" rule outside the narrative homepage.

## 6. Copy system

### Voice rules

- Short headline, clear support line, then proof.
- Prefer active verbs and concrete outcomes.
- Be confident about the work, modest about AG.
- One thought per screen. Keep major headlines to 2-7 words.
- Avoid mystical language, empty superlatives and invented performance claims.
- Use the dimensional metaphor sparingly. Not every sentence needs space, gravity or portals.

## 7. Visual direction

### Colour world

- Base: deep blue-black ink, approximately `#070814`.
- Primary energy: vivid violet, approximately `#7456FF`.
- Secondary energy: clear cyan, approximately `#28B7FF`.
- Human warmth: controlled coral or soft amber, used mainly in production/team moments.
- Text: warm off-white rather than pure white.
- Every colour needs a non-colour cue when it conveys state.

The supplied deck already introduces dark ink, violet and cyan. Develop these into a controlled system rather than a constant luminous gradient.

### Lighting and material language

- Use narrow area lights, gradient environment lighting and selective emissive edges.
- Favour frosted acrylic, anodised metal, translucent film, coated paper and polished polymer.
- Keep bloom local and low. Black levels must retain detail.
- Portfolio images use colour-managed, unlit or carefully lit surfaces so brand work is reproduced accurately.
- Add a subtle paper/noise texture in the HTML layer to prevent sterile CGI, but keep it below 2 percent contrast.

### Typography

- Use the client's licensed brand typeface if one exists.
- Pair a contemporary grotesk variable font with an unobtrusive mono or tabular style for coordinates and progress.
- Treat typography as spatial content only when a corresponding HTML copy exists.
- Avoid long curved text, tiny labels and perspective distortion on essential messaging.

### Composition

- Reserve at least one calm reading zone in every scene.
- Alternate dense and sparse scenes so spectacle has rhythm.
- Use asymmetry early, alignment later.
- Keep the main object large and memorable. Do not fill every depth plane.

## 8. 3D asset and production plan

### Essential asset set

| Asset family | Use | Construction | Variants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fragment kit | Scene 1 and transitions | Instanced glyph shards, image planes, line fragments and chips | 25-40 source forms, instanced |
| A aperture | Scene 2 | Exact approved A SVG path with pixel-faithful front silhouette and shallow controlled extrusion | desktop and simplified mobile |
| Tesseract rail system | Scenes 3-12 | Modular rail, node and membrane kit | open, folded and resolved states |
| Strategy instrument | Scene 4 | Axes, vector nodes, constraint planes | low-poly procedural |
| Identity modules | Scene 5 | Type planes, colour fields, mark fragments, grids | CMS-driven artwork surfaces |
| Signal network | Scene 6 | Instanced nodes, spline paths, pulse shader | desktop and reduced node counts |
| Content timeline | Scene 7 | Frame planes, text supers and edit markers | still, video-preview and poster modes |
| Matter Lab | Scene 8 | Plinth, light cards, lens planes, subject frames | simplified mobile stage |
| Portal kit | Scene 9 | Reusable frames, masks and depth layers | up to three portal silhouettes |
| Project artefacts | Scene 10 | Image/video frames plus project-specific hero forms | 3-5 launch projects |
| Human planes | Scene 11 | Portrait/process surfaces and annotation lines | responsive crops |
| G resolution rig | Scene 12 | Exact approved G SVG path used as the alignment target for reusable rails and arcs | forming G, full monogram and still lockup |
| Full logo lockup | Header, footer and final frame | Untouched supplied SVG including the curved `designs` detail | full and approved monogram-only responsive state |

### Optional enhancement assets

- Custom signed-distance-field typography shader.
- Soft volumetric light around the aperture.
- One project-specific sculptural hero per flagship case study.
- Generative sound layer driven by scene state.
- WebGPU progressive enhancement after WebGL production stability, never as launch dependency.

### Asset pipeline

1. Block every scene with primitive geometry before custom modelling.
2. Approve camera path and narrative readability in grayscale.
3. Build modular assets in Blender at real scale with consistent axes and pivots.
4. Export glTF/GLB. Prefer instancing and shared materials.
5. Optimise meshes with Meshopt or Draco where it produces a meaningful saving.
6. Convert colour textures to KTX2/Basis; keep normal maps only where their visual contribution is visible.
7. Generate explicit mobile variants. Do not rely only on runtime decimation.
8. Validate colour space, tangent data, animation clips, bounding boxes and disposal behavior.
9. Store source `.blend` and working textures separately from delivery `.glb` and `.ktx2` files.

Three.js's current glTF loader supports Draco, Meshopt and KTX2 integration, making glTF 2.0 the appropriate exchange and delivery format for this production ([Three.js GLTFLoader documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html)).

### Runtime geometry budgets

| Budget | High desktop | Balanced tablet/laptop | Mobile light |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Visible triangles | <= 500k | <= 280k | <= 120k |
| Draw calls per frame | <= 100 | <= 75 | <= 50 |
| Active lights | 3-5 | 2-3 | 1-2 |
| Max texture dimension | 2048 px | 2048 px selective | 1024 px |
| Device pixel ratio | adaptive, max 1.75 | adaptive, max 1.5 | adaptive, max 1.25 |
| Post-processing | selective bloom + subtle grain | bloom only | none or minimal |

These are starting gates. Measure on representative devices and lower complexity before sacrificing navigation or copy.

## 9. Motion and interaction rules

### Core motion vocabulary

Every transition should use one of four verbs:

- **Disperse:** the unresolved state.
- **Attract:** AG finds a relationship.
- **Align:** strategy creates order.
- **Fold:** the system reveals another dimension without losing continuity.

### Scroll

- Use native vertical scroll as the input and a labelled GSAP timeline as the story clock.
- Do not hijack wheel distance or remove the browser scrollbar.
- Recommended smoothing for scrubbed animation: 0.35-0.6 seconds, tuned after testing.
- Each scene gets a semantic HTML section and a timeline label.
- Fast scrolling must resolve to a stable scene state rather than leaving half-visible copy.
- Backward scrolling reverses the visual logic cleanly.
- GSAP ScrollTrigger is suitable because it can scrub, pin, respond to velocity, recalculate on resize and use breakpoint-specific setups while preserving native scrolling ([official ScrollTrigger documentation](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)).

### Camera

- Use a authored spline or explicit camera keyframes, not free-fly controls.
- Maximum roll: 3 degrees except the brief dimensional fold, which still keeps text level in the HTML layer.
- Avoid rapid forward acceleration, oscillation and long tunnel effects.
- Keep primary focus distance stable during copy beats.
- Camera is controlled only by scene progress. Pointer adds a small child-rig offset and never changes the actual story position.

### Pointer and touch

- Pointer parallax: maximum 2 degrees rotation or 12 px apparent displacement.
- Cursor state changes communicate action: default, focus, enter, open proof.
- Use magnetic attraction only within 24-40 px of a control and never move the actual hit target.
- Touch uses direct taps and scroll velocity. No interaction depends on hover.
- Drag is optional and always has button alternatives, in line with WCAG 2.2 input requirements.

### Typography in motion

- Entrance: 300-550 ms, mostly opacity plus 8-16 px translation.
- Exit: 200-400 ms.
- Avoid character-by-character animation for body copy.
- Do not blur text during reading states.
- Keep HTML copy visually anchored even when its matching 3D object moves.

### Sound

- Muted by default; enable only after an explicit user gesture.
- Use one continuous ambient bed with scene-dependent filters rather than a new sound effect for every object.
- Functional actions may use short, quiet confirmations.
- Persist the sound preference locally.
- Provide a visible control and never make audio necessary to understand the story.

### Loading

- Show the HTML shell immediately.
- Load only the aperture and reveal assets before starting the immersive route.
- Lazy-load later scene packages 1-2 scenes ahead.
- Replace fake percentage progress with actual asset-manager progress or use an indeterminate phrase.

## 11. Responsive, reduced-motion and accessibility strategy

### Experience tiers

Use capability detection, not user-agent detection alone.

#### Tier A - Full cinematic

- Large screen, adequate GPU and memory, no reduced-motion preference.
- Full camera path, adaptive post-processing, pointer reactions and optional sound.

#### Tier B - Balanced 3D

- Tablets, integrated GPUs and constrained laptops.
- Same story and scene count, simplified meshes, fewer particles, reduced effects and lower DPR.

#### Tier C - Mobile light

- One persistent simplified canvas or a sequence of 2.5D scene compositions.
- No volumetrics, no heavy depth of field, no tiny hotspots.
- Scroll distance reduced by 25-35 percent.
- Project previews open as bottom sheets or routes.

#### Tier D - Semantic fallback

Activated for `prefers-reduced-motion`, Save-Data, no WebGL, repeated context loss or user selection.

- Render every scene as accessible HTML with approved still images or short user-controlled video loops.
- No parallax, auto-rotation, pinned tunnel or scroll-scrubbed camera.
- Preserve the same copy order, capability links, project links and CTA.
- The W3C specifically recommends honoring `prefers-reduced-motion` to suppress interaction-triggered motion that may cause distraction or nausea ([W3C Technique C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39)).

### Accessibility requirements

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for all content and controls ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).
- A visible skip link moves to main content.
- All essential copy is DOM text in logical heading order.
- Canvas is decorative or has a concise label; never duplicate dozens of objects into the accessibility tree.
- All hotspots have keyboard-equivalent HTML controls.
- Focus is clearly visible and never obscured by fixed navigation.
- Minimum target size: 24 by 24 CSS px, preferably 44 by 44 on touch.
- Text contrast meets 4.5:1 for normal copy and 3:1 for large text and meaningful UI graphics.
- No flashing above safe thresholds.
- Pause/stop controls exist for any independently moving content lasting more than 5 seconds.
- Video has captions; meaningful audio has a transcript or equivalent copy.
- Form errors are associated with fields and announced.
- Test VoiceOver + Safari, NVDA + Chrome, keyboard-only, 200 percent zoom and 320 CSS px reflow.

## 12. Recommended technical stack

Use current stable releases at project kickoff and pin exact versions in the lockfile after the first technical spike.

### Application

- **Next.js App Router + React + TypeScript.** Server-render the semantic content, metadata and case studies; isolate the immersive renderer as a client-only island. App Router supports Server Components, Suspense and file-based routing ([Next.js App Router documentation](https://nextjs.org/docs/app)).
- **React Three Fiber + Three.js + Drei.** Use R3F for declarative scene composition and Three.js for loaders, shaders and lower-level controls.
- **GSAP + ScrollTrigger.** One master timeline coordinates DOM, camera and scene state.
- **Zustand.** Small explicit store for experience mode, active scene, sound preference, quality tier and overlay state. Do not store every animation value in React state.
- **CSS custom properties + CSS Modules or Tailwind utilities.** Keep design tokens central and cinematic layout rules explicit. Avoid a large component-library aesthetic.

### Content and operations

- **Sanity** as the recommended CMS, with the official `next-sanity` integration, draft mode and visual editing. Its current Next.js tooling is built for App Router, Server Components and live preview ([Sanity Next.js documentation](https://www.sanity.io/docs/nextjs/introduction)).
- **Vercel** for hosting if the team wants the simplest Next.js deployment, preview URLs and image delivery. A CDN-compatible Node host is also acceptable.
- **Sentry** for runtime errors and WebGL context-loss visibility.
- **Privacy-conscious analytics** or GA4, with explicit events and consent appropriate to the launch market.

### Quality tooling

- ESLint, TypeScript strict mode and Prettier.
- Vitest + React Testing Library for logic and component tests.
- Playwright for routes, keyboard flows, fallbacks and smoke tests.
- axe-core for automated accessibility checks, followed by manual assistive-technology testing.
- Lighthouse CI and real-user Web Vitals reporting.
- Blender, glTF Transform, KTX2/Basis and Meshopt/Draco for the asset pipeline.

### Avoid

- A second scroll-smoothing engine unless the technical spike proves it is necessary.
- Multiple canvases for every scene.
- Putting CMS fetches, long copy or form behavior inside the renderer.
- Shipping one monolithic GLB for the whole homepage.
- Using WebGPU-only features for launch.
- Building the experience as a video disguised as a website.

## 14. Performance targets and budgets

### User-facing targets

- Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms, CLS <= 0.1, measured separately for mobile and desktop. These are the published "good" thresholds ([Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)).
- First meaningful HTML headline: <= 1.5 s on a mid-tier mobile device over Fast 4G.
- First useful interaction: <= 2.5 s.
- First immersive frame: <= 4.0 s on the same profile; fallback is already usable before this.
- Sustained frame rate: target 60 fps desktop, 45+ balanced devices, never remain below 30 fps for more than 2 seconds.
- Main-thread long tasks: no repeated tasks over 50 ms during scroll.
- Lighthouse targets on representative production builds: Performance >= 80 full immersive mobile and >= 90 fallback; Accessibility >= 95; Best Practices >= 95; SEO >= 95.

### Initial transfer budgets

- Initial route JavaScript, Brotli: <= 300 KB before deferred WebGL packages; <= 550 KB including the minimum immersive runtime.
- Critical CSS, Brotli: <= 50 KB.
- Fonts: <= 120 KB total initial, with only required weights/subsets.
- Above-fold imagery/poster: <= 250 KB AVIF/WebP.
- Initial 3D assets required for Scenes 1-3: <= 3.0 MB compressed desktop, <= 1.2 MB mobile.
- Total homepage 3D payload if the entire story is completed: <= 10 MB compressed desktop, <= 4 MB mobile, loaded progressively.
- Do not preload full case-study videos. Use posters and load on intent.

### Adaptive quality policy

1. Read reduced-motion, Save-Data and WebGL availability before loading the renderer.
2. Begin conservatively on unknown devices.
3. Sample frame time for 2-3 seconds after stable interaction.
4. If median frame time exceeds budget, reduce DPR, particles and post-processing in that order.
5. If context is lost twice or performance remains below 24 fps, switch to the fallback and preserve the current scene.
6. Send anonymous performance tier and fallback reason to analytics only with appropriate consent.

## 3D implementation rules

Until final art assets are supplied, build visually intentional procedural placeholders from primitives, instanced geometry, lines, splines, image planes and restrained shaders. Do not download random stock 3D models. Make asset replacement straightforward.

The logo is not a placeholder. Treat the supplied SVG paths as immutable production geometry from the first vertical slice:

- Use cropped samples of the real A angles and G curve as early abstract fragments, without revealing the complete G.
- Use the exact A counter-space for the aperture.
- Derive recurring tesseract right angles and rectangular proportions from the A.
- Align final rails/arcs to the exact G path before resolving the full monogram.
- Keep isolated A and G scene groups on stable documented pivots and scale.
- Use the full untouched SVG for the still final lockup; do not reconstruct the full logo from approximate 3D pieces.

- glTF/GLB for authored geometry.
- Meshopt or Draco only after measuring savings.
- KTX2/Basis for colour textures.
- Reuse geometry/materials and use instancing.
- Lazy-load one or two scenes ahead.
- Generate real mobile variants rather than only shrinking desktop assets.
- Correctly dispose geometries, materials, textures, render targets and listeners.
- No monolithic GLB.
- No WebGPU-only launch dependency.

Initial visual palette: deep ink `#070814`, vivid violet near `#7456FF`, clear cyan near `#28B7FF`, warm off-white text and a restrained warm accent in production/team scenes. Use frosted acrylic, anodised metal, translucent film, coated paper and polished polymer. Bloom must be local and subtle.

## Motion rules

- Native vertical scroll drives one GSAP timeline with named labels.
- Scrub smoothing starts around 0.35-0.6 seconds and is tuned by testing.
- Camera uses an authored path or keyframes. No free-fly controls.
- Maximum normal camera roll is 3 degrees.
- Pointer parallax is bounded to about 2 degrees or 12 px apparent displacement.
- Pointer only affects a child rig, never the story position.
- No essential hover-only interaction.
- Text is HTML, not WebGL-only. Keep copy stable while it is being read.
- Major text entrance 300-550 ms; exit 200-400 ms; no long per-character body animation.
- Fast and reverse scrolling must resolve coherently.

## Responsive and accessibility modes

Implement capability-based modes:

1. `full`: desktop/high capability, full effects.
2. `balanced`: tablet/integrated GPU, reduced geometry/DPR/effects.
3. `mobile`: simplified canvas or 2.5D compositions, no heavy post-processing, shorter scroll distances.
4. `reduced`: honors `prefers-reduced-motion`; removes scroll-scrubbed camera motion, parallax and non-essential animation.
5. `fallback`: Save-Data, no WebGL, repeated context loss or user-selected semantic mode.

All modes must preserve the same copy hierarchy, service links, project links and CTA. Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Include a skip link, logical headings, visible focus, accessible menus/dialogs/forms, captions, alt text, keyboard-equivalent controls, 200 percent zoom support and 320 CSS px reflow. Canvas must never be the only place essential information exists.

## Performance gates

Treat these as acceptance gates, not aspirations:

- LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms and CLS <= 0.1 at p75.
- Initial HTML headline <= 1.5 s and first useful interaction <= 2.5 s on a mid-tier mobile/Fast 4G profile.
- First immersive frame <= 4.0 s while the fallback is already usable.
- Target 60 fps desktop, 45+ fps balanced devices, 30 fps minimum floor.
- Initial JS Brotli <= 300 KB before deferred WebGL packages and <= 550 KB including the minimum immersive runtime.
- Initial Scene 1-3 3D payload <= 3.0 MB desktop and <= 1.2 MB mobile.
- Total progressively loaded homepage 3D <= 10 MB desktop and <= 4 MB mobile.
- Visible geometry guide: <= 500k triangles/100 draw calls desktop; <= 120k triangles/50 draw calls mobile.
- Dynamically lower DPR, particles and post-processing when frame time misses budget.
- After repeated context loss or sustained performance below 24 fps, preserve the active scene and switch to fallback.

Add bundle/asset size checks and Lighthouse CI. Do not claim the gates pass unless you ran the relevant production tests.
